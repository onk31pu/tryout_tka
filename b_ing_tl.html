4 Identifying the Central Argument PG Regular

For years, schools have treated smartphones primarily as distractions. Consequently, many institutions have adopted strict policies prohibiting students from using them during lessons. However, recent discussions have shifted toward a more nuanced approach. Rather than eliminating smartphones altogether, some educators argue that schools should teach students how to use digital devices responsibly.

A smartphone, for instance, can provide immediate access to dictionaries, scientific databases, educational videos, and collaborative platforms. Yet the same device can also fragment students' attention through notifications and social media. The issue, therefore, may not be the technology itself but the user's ability to regulate its use.

Schools that integrate smartphones into learning must establish clear boundaries. Without guidance, students may simply replace one form of distraction with another. Digital literacy, in this context, involves not only knowing how to access information but also knowing when, why, and whether such information should be used.

8 Classifying Statements: Agree / Disagree PG Kategori
Do Electric Vehicles Guarantee a Sustainable Future?

The transition from gasoline-powered cars to electric vehicles (EVs) is often presented as an important step toward a more sustainable future. Governments, automobile manufacturers, and environmental organizations have promoted EVs because they produce no direct exhaust emissions while being driven. In crowded cities, this characteristic can contribute to cleaner air and reduce people's exposure to certain pollutants.

However, calling EVs completely "green" may be an oversimplification. The environmental impact of an electric vehicle begins long before it reaches the road. Its battery requires minerals such as lithium, nickel, and cobalt, which must be extracted and processed. Mining these materials can consume significant amounts of water and energy and, if poorly regulated, can damage ecosystems and affect local communities. The source of electricity is another important consideration. An EV charged using electricity generated largely from coal may still indirectly contribute to greenhouse gas emissions. In contrast, an EV powered by electricity from solar, wind, or other low-carbon sources can have a considerably smaller environmental footprint over its lifetime. Thus, the sustainability of EVs depends partly on the broader energy system in which they operate.

Battery disposal also presents a challenge. When batteries are no longer suitable for powering vehicles, simply throwing them away would waste valuable materials and could create environmental risks. Fortunately, battery recycling technologies are developing rapidly. Some used batteries can also be given a second life as energy-storage systems before their materials are eventually recycled.

For these reasons, electric vehicles should not be regarded as a magic solution to climate change. They can play a significant role in reducing transportation-related emissions, but their effectiveness depends on responsible mineral extraction, cleaner electricity, longer-lasting batteries, effective recycling systems, and complementary forms of sustainable transportation such as public transit and cycling. Ultimately, the question is not whether electric vehicles are perfectly sustainable. Rather, the more important question is whether societies can develop the infrastructure, policies, and technologies necessary to make electric transportation increasingly sustainable.

1) Electric vehicles can contribute to cleaner air in densely populated cities.

10 Summarizing the Author's Position PG Regular

Artificial intelligence is increasingly being used to assist students in writing. Some educators welcome this development because AI can provide immediate feedback on grammar, organization, and clarity. Others worry that students may become dependent on automated assistance and gradually lose the ability to develop their own ideas.

The disagreement, however, may be based on a false choice. Students do not necessarily have to choose between writing independently and using AI. The more important question is how AI is incorporated into the writing process. If students use AI to replace thinking, the technology may weaken learning. If they use it to challenge their assumptions, identify weaknesses, and revise their work, it may strengthen learning.

Thus, the educational value of AI depends less on its presence than on the intellectual role students assign to it.

These actions can be meaningful, but focusing exclusively on them may obscure the role of larger systems. 23 True / False — AI in Education PG Kategori
Artificial Intelligence in Education

Artificial intelligence has rapidly entered classrooms, offering students tools that can provide instant explanations, generate examples, and give feedback on written work. Supporters argue that such tools can make learning more personalized because students can ask questions repeatedly without feeling embarrassed. However, easy access to AI also creates a potential problem. If students rely on AI to produce answers instead of engaging with difficult ideas themselves, they may complete assignments successfully without developing deeper understanding. Therefore, educators increasingly emphasize that AI should function as a learning assistant rather than a substitute for students' intellectual effort. The challenge is not simply deciding whether AI belongs in education. Instead, schools need to establish practices that encourage students to question, verify, and critically evaluate AI-generated information.
